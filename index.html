Customer Connection: Kelvin Cave Barber Shop

A simple, branded customer registration experience designed to help a neighbourhood barbershop collect customer details, communicate through preferred channels and give customers the option to connect with its online community.

  • Branded customer registration
  • WhatsApp and email communication options
  • Separate marketing consent
  • Optional WhatsApp community invitation
  • Instagram connection (planned, not yet live)

An early pilot demonstration of how we identify a gap and develop a practical solution. It has no central database or automated analytics, and we make no claims about retention, sales or revenue.

Explore the Project

Simplifying Customer Connections for a Neighbourhood Barbershop.

Early-stage pilot. Not a proven business outcome.

Business context
Kelvin Cave is a local barber shop that serves walk-in and returning customers.
Opportunity identified
A shop can learn who its customers are and reach them again, if there is an easy way to connect.
The challenge (our working hypothesis)
Customers can leave without the shop having a way to follow up. We are testing this assumption, not asserting it.
The proposed solution
A short registration form that sends details to the shop and offers optional channels to stay connected.
The customer journey
Open the link, fill in the form, choose consent options, send by WhatsApp or email, then optionally join the community group.
The technology
A static web page. It stores nothing and has no central database. Details travel in a message the customer sends.
What we are testing
Whether customers complete the form, and whether the shop finds the message format useful.
Lessons and next steps
Add the Instagram connection, gather the shop’s feedback and decide what to improve next.

Improving engagement doesn’t always call for a large system. A simple, well-designed step, used consistently, can matter more than a powerful tool nobody has time to learn.

The difference between acquiring customers and retaining themCustomer Experience · 12 Nov 2026

Acquiring a customer and keeping one are different problems with different solutions. Treating them as the same thing can hide where a business is actually losing value.

How small operational improvements can improve customer experienceBusiness Operations · 12 Nov 2026

Customers rarely see a business’s internal processes, but they feel them. Small fixes, like fewer steps to register or a clearer way to reach the shop, often show up as a better experience.
